# ISO 9241-11:2018: usability outcomes in context

Use when defining success measures or planning and reporting a usability evaluation.

## Source and scope

[ISO 9241-11:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/63500.html), edition 2, remains current after confirmation in 2023. It treats usability as an outcome of use in specified circumstances, not a property established by a polished screenshot. The public [contents, page iii](https://webstore.ansi.org/preview-pages/ISO/preview_ISO%2B9241-11-2018.pdf#page=3), identify effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction in sections 6.2-6.4, with context covered in section 7.

The catalog expressly does not prescribe specific design or evaluation methods. The operational checks below are our application, not ISO definitions, thresholds, or a conformance assessment. Public sources checked 2026-11-15; the licensed full standard was not reviewed or bundled.

## Specify the context before measuring

- **Apply:** name the user group, goal, task, environment, available resources, and relevant constraints. Choose representative participants and scenarios; record unsupported contexts instead of generalizing one result to everyone.
- **Example:** test whether a first-time administrator can identify a blocked permission and understand its cause using a keyboard on a narrow viewport.
- **Check:** attach the scenario and participant/context limits to every reported result. Separate observed behavior from assumptions about other users or devices.

## Effectiveness

- **Apply:** define correct, complete task outcomes and meaningful errors before testing. Observe what changed in the system as well as what the participant believes happened.
- **Example:** success requires changing only the intended agent's access and accurately identifying its resulting effective permission.
- **Check:** record correct completions, partial completions, errors, and assistance. A fast but incorrect result is not success.

## Efficiency

- **Apply:** assess resources spent relative to the outcome, such as time and effort. Define task start/end and assistance rules consistently; interpret speed alongside accuracy rather than minimizing clicks indiscriminately.
- **Example:** compare time and unnecessary backtracking for the same correctly completed permission task before and after a redesign.
- **Check:** report the sample, conditions, and spread of results. Separate incomplete attempts and avoid treating one timing as a universal benchmark.

## Satisfaction

- **Apply:** gather users' reported comfort, confidence, and experience in the task context. Keep these reports distinct from completion and timing evidence.
- **Example:** after the task, ask how confident the participant is about the effective access and what made the workflow easy or difficult.
- **Check:** retain the question wording and reported answers. Investigate cases where confidence is high but the result is wrong, or success requires frustrating effort.

For the iterative design process, load [ISO 9241-210](iso-9241-210.md). For diagnosing interaction and presentation issues, load [ISO 9241-110](iso-9241-110.md) and [ISO 9241-112](iso-9241-112.md). Usability measurements complement, rather than replace, [WCAG evaluation](wcag-22.md).
