# ISO 9241-112: presentation of information

Use for hierarchy, grouping, labels, context, and interpretation of state.

## The six presentation principles

This is the complete principle-heading set in **ISO 9241-112:2025** order, clauses 6.1-6.6, verified in the [ANSI-authorized preview, contents page iii (PDF page 3)](https://webstore.ansi.org/preview-pages/ISO/preview_ISO%2B9241-112-2025.pdf#page=3). Each clause's `.1` subsection contains the principle statement; its later subsections contain supporting guidance, not additional principles.

Only the short headings below are ISO wording. **Apply**, **Example**, and **Check** are this playbook's original operational interpretation, not ISO definitions, clause text, or a conformance assessment. Examples are illustrative product scenarios, not claims about an existing implementation.

### 6.1 Detectability

- **Apply:** make task-relevant information noticeable at the time and place it is needed, including changes in state.
- **Example:** keep the selected agent and folder scope above their settings; show a failed update beside the affected permission rather than only in a distant notification.
- **Check:** ask the user to locate the current scope and any new exception during the task; record missed information and whether the presentation made it hard to find.

### 6.2 Discriminability

- **Apply:** make different information, objects, and states distinguishable through clear structure and cues rather than color alone.
- **Example:** display a requested permission separately from effective access, with labels and a blocking reason when the two differ.
- **Check:** ask the user to identify which state is selected, pending, inherited, or blocked; repeat without relying on color to distinguish the answers.

### 6.3 Conciseness

- **Apply:** present the information needed for the decision with no unnecessary repetition or effort, while keeping important consequences available.
- **Example:** place a brief blocking reason beside a setting and put its policy history in a clearly labeled detail view instead of repeating the history in every row.
- **Check:** identify content the user must read or actions they must take that add nothing to the task; verify that removing or disclosing details does not hide a needed consequence.

### 6.4 Unambiguous interpretability

- **Apply:** give information a clear meaning in its context, including what a value or state represents and why it applies.
- **Example:** show "Edit Document selected; effective access blocked by the destination's read-only policy" rather than an unexplained "Restricted" badge. Distinguish a real zero from missing or stale data.
- **Check:** ask the user to explain what applies now and why, then compare that explanation with the actual state and policy.

### 6.5 Freedom from distraction

- **Apply:** keep irrelevant motion, competing emphasis, and interruptions from pulling attention away from the current task; retain necessary alerts and visible controls.
- **Example:** ordinary rows use restrained styling while an actionable exception receives a clear message. Routine background refreshes do not repeatedly flash or interrupt editing.
- **Check:** observe whether unrelated changes divert the user or cause lost progress; verify that important exceptions remain noticeable and accessible.

### 6.6 Consistency (internal and external)

- **Apply:** use compatible words, grouping, symbols, and presentation conventions across the product and with conventions familiar to its users.
- **Example:** every editor places selected scope and effective state in the same relative positions and uses the same label for the same kind of restriction.
- **Check:** compare equivalent information across screens and with relevant domain/platform conventions; record contradictory terms, placements, or cues.

## Source and scope

[ISO 9241-112:2025](https://www.iso.org/standard/87518.html), edition 2, was published in June 2025 and replaces the withdrawn 2017 edition. Its scope concerns perception and understanding of software-presented information across visual, auditory, and tactile/haptic output. Sources and heading order verified 2026-11-15.

Use the 2025 heading set above. The licensed standard is needed for the actual principle statements, supporting recommendations, and clause-level evaluation. Requested, effective, inherited, direct, blocked, and unsaved are useful product categories in these examples, not ISO-defined state names.

## Review evidence

For every principle, record the relevant information/state, observation, and result: pass, fail, not tested, or not applicable with a reason. A complete heading list and completed local checks do not establish ISO conformance.

Use [ISO 9241-110's interaction principles](iso-9241-110.md#the-seven-interaction-principles) for control behavior and [WCAG](wcag-22.md) for perceptibility and programmatic semantics. Understandable visual grouping alone does not demonstrate accessibility.
