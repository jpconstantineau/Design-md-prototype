# IEC 62682:2022: alarm management and operator response

Use for monitoring interfaces, notification centers, alarm priorities, response context, and claims about alarm-management conformance.

## Source and scope

[IEC 62682:2022](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/65543), *Management of alarm systems for the process industries*, is **edition 2.0**, published **8 December 2022**. IEC's public overview covers industrial alarm systems for continuous, batch, and discrete processes. It describes notifying operators about abnormal conditions and supporting their response, with HMI communication, event records, history, and performance measures. It does not govern general consumer web pages or mobile push notifications.

The [CMV product](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/80816) packages that edition with expert comments and comparison to 2014; it is not a newer edition. Public summaries do not prescribe project hex values or validate priority mappings. The full licensed text was not reviewed or bundled. Sources checked 2026-11-15.

The practices below are **original web adaptations**, not IEC clauses or a complete industrial lifecycle. [ISA's public alarm-management explanation](https://www.isa.org/intech-home/2020/march-april/features/alarm-management-questions-that-everyone-asks) corroborates separating actionable abnormalities from awareness notifications and documenting consequences, response time, and action. Its discussion predates IEC's 2022 edition; do not treat the standards as interchangeable. Industrial decisions need the applicable full text and responsible domain specialists.

## Actionable alerts, not routine noise

- **Apply:** distinguish an abnormal condition needing a timely action to avoid a consequence from ordinary status, successful completion, or marketing. Define which person can take which action before choosing an interruptive presentation.
- **Example:** a hypothetical indexing service alerts when a stalled job threatens a documented deadline; a completed run appears in activity history rather than as a high-priority alarm.
- **Check:** for each alert type, identify the abnormal condition, consequence, responder, and time available. Reclassify informational items that have no timely response.

## Priority from consequence and response window

- **Apply:** define project priorities using both impact and time available to respond. Keep the most urgent class exceptional by removing unjustified alerts, not by downgrading real hazards to meet a quota. Choose names and tiers for the project; this guide mandates no count or ratio.
- **Example:** an imminent loss of an essential service and a distant capacity concern receive different response windows even when both concern the same subsystem.
- **Check:** trace representative assignments to documented rules and review a burst of events. Confirm the genuinely urgent item remains findable without relying on its color.

## Reduce repeats without masking new problems

[ISA's implementation article](https://www.isa.org/intech-home/2019/january-february/features/applying-alarm-management) distinguishes basic deadband/delay techniques from more complex approaches that are useful only in selected cases. The following choices need task-specific validation:

- **Apply:** group or deduplicate repeated reports of the same condition while retaining counts, timestamps, and distinct affected objects. For threshold signals, a deadband uses different activation and recovery thresholds; a persistence delay waits for a condition to last. They are not synonyms. Choose either only when safe, and let urgent critical events bypass generic batching or delay that would consume their response window.
- **Example:** a fluctuating capacity warning has an approved recovery threshold; repeated reports update one visible incident. An independently detected critical failure still appears immediately.
- **Check:** replay threshold oscillations, repeats, recovery, and a distinct simultaneous failure. Verify meaningful events survive grouping, history remains inspectable, and delay leaves enough time to respond.

## Time-limited, auditable snoozing

- **Apply:** offer snoozing only for authorized roles and eligible alerts under an explicit policy. Show who snoozed what, why, and until when; provide an inspectable snoozed list and automatic expiry. Protect critical alerts according to policy rather than silently hiding them. Snoozing does not resolve the condition or disable detection.
- **Example:** an authorized responder temporarily snoozes a known non-critical repeat with a reason. A newly escalated critical condition bypasses that snooze under the project's rule.
- **Check:** test unauthorized requests, expiry, escalation, and early restoration. Confirm the audit record survives and the active condition becomes visible again when the snooze ends.

## Evidence and action beside the alert

- **Apply:** show what happened, the affected object, time, possible consequence, and recommended next action. Distinguish an established cause from a hypothesis; use "unknown" when evidence is missing. Link relevant diagnostics or trends when they help the response.
- **Example:** "Source unavailable" includes its last successful check, impact on the current run, and a connection-test action. It does not invent a network cause from the symptom.
- **Check:** ask a user to explain the condition and next action without guessing. Verify that instructions match their permissions and that any cause claim has evidence.

## Redundant visual coding

- **Apply:** pair priority text with consistent icons and semantic color, using [WCAG](wcag-22.md) for actual contrast, accessible names, and status announcements. Ensure information remains understandable without pulsing, animation, sound, or color alone. Reserve forceful interruption for the response need.
- **Example:** an urgent row has a priority label and recognizable symbol; a reduced-motion view conveys the same meaning without a pulsing badge.
- **Check:** inspect monochrome and reduced-motion views, keyboard focus, and screen-reader output. Confirm urgent information is available without turning every update into an assertive announcement.

## Active summaries, independent states, and history

The [OPC Foundation's public alarm model](https://reference.opcfoundation.org/specs/OPC-10000-9/4.8) separates activity from acknowledgement-related states; its [acknowledgement discussion](https://reference.opcfoundation.org/specs/OPC-10000-9/4.3) allows implementation-specific transitions. This corroborates the distinction, not a required web state machine.

- **Apply:** provide a persistent, readily reachable active summary with useful sort/filter controls and visible filter scope. Represent condition state, acknowledgement, and snooze separately. Support active/unacknowledged, active/acknowledged, and cleared/unacknowledged where the workflow requires them; acknowledgement is not repair. Keep a separate searchable history/audit view with event identity and state-change times, and retain cleared items needing attention in an appropriate queue.
- **Example:** acknowledging a failed job marks it seen while the failure remains active. Recovery changes the condition state, while the event and acknowledgement stay in history. If it recovered before anyone saw it, an outstanding acknowledgement can remain.
- **Check:** verify that acknowledging does not clear the condition, clearing does not erase history, and filters do not silently conceal urgent items. Exercise cleared-but-unacknowledged, recurrence, and refresh/reconnect paths.
